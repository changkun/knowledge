# Knowledge: Teaching and Course Design

[![中文](https://img.shields.io/badge/Language-%E4%B8%AD%E6%96%87-0A66C2)](zh/teaching-and-course-design.md)

Teaching is a major site where knowledge is output over the long term, value judgments are tested, and professional identity is shaped.

Within the overall structure, this page is the first part of the output layer. Knowledge no longer remains at the level of personal understanding, but is reorganized into courses, evaluation schemes, and training structures.

The focus here is how teaching organizes knowledge and developmental paths. How those materials circulate publicly beyond the classroom is handled by the neighboring page.

## Teaching as Part of Long-Term Work

One key line in [changkun.de/blog/posts/2021-01-03-2020-summary/](https://changkun.de/blog/posts/2021-01-03-2020-summary/) is that doctoral life simultaneously included reading, writing papers, teaching, industry collaboration, and project work.

That makes teaching a formal core responsibility rather than an afterthought.

## Scale and Roles

- Long-term work at LMU as research assistant and lecturer from 2018 to 2023, with teaching resumed again after 2024.
- Teaching across 12 courses in roles including lecturer, course instructor, teaching assistant, and tutor, with broad scope and long service cycles.
- Formal supervision of 20+ theses and seminars, plus another 18 students brought into research training on an ongoing basis.
- Teaching here has moved from "explaining what one already knows" into long-term talent development and research organization.

Public course repositories on GitHub reinforce this. The `teaching` repository works like a long-maintained course entry point, organizing Geometry Processing, Computer Graphics, Online Multimedia, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning and Artificial Intelligence into slides, assignments, notebooks, lecture notes, and exercises as public material.

## Several Clear Teaching Lines

- **Machine learning / deep learning tutoring**
  The 2018 summary already shows that machine-learning and deep-learning tutoring took substantial effort, with preparation done by working through concepts one by one.
  The `ss18-machine-learning-tutorial` and `ws-18-19-deep-learning-tutorial` repositories further show week-by-week notebooks, handwritten material, and solution paths that already form a complete body of teaching material.

- **Public tutorials on mathematical modeling**
  [changkun.de/blog/math-modeling/](https://changkun.de/blog/math-modeling/) shows that teaching also deliberately moves outside the classroom, turning probability, modeling, simulation, Markov chains, and similar topics into a continuous video tutorial series.

- **Rebuilding the Online Multimedia course**
  The 2020 summary shows a clear effort to replace outdated content with a more modern web view, bringing React, system design, Docker, and Kubernetes into the course.

- **Computer Graphics**
  This is the most systematic teaching output. It builds a near-complete path from mathematics, transformation, geometry, and cameras to rasterization, materials, lighting, and review.
  The structures in `cg1ss20-internal`, `cg1ss21-internal`, and `teaching` also show a full course system organized around weekly slides, assignments, grading, and review material.

- **Geometry Processing**
  In the second half of 2020, teaching continues into a more advanced geometry-processing course. The smaller enrollment also reflects higher difficulty and specialization.
  The GP section in `teaching`, from discrete differential geometry to remeshing assignments, shows a concern with leading students into research-grade and implementation-grade problems rather than merely covering topics.

- **Pedagogy and academic expression**
  The unpublished drafts `What Should We Talk About When We Give Academic Presentations?` and `An Analysis and Discussion of Teaching-Quality Evaluation` show attention to what to teach, how to present, how to evaluate, and how to organize classroom and academic expression.

## Teaching Philosophy

- **High knowledge density over easy consumption**
  There is no sympathy for aggressively diluting content in the name of low friction.

- **Structured paths**
  Courses are organized as complete paths from prerequisites to project work, with dependencies kept explicit.

- **Practical implementation**
  Many courses require students to build real projects rather than only memorize concepts.

- **Responsibility**
  Even under pressure from poor evaluations, complaints, or pass-rate constraints, the tendency is to hold the line that course content should be useful and serious.

- **Course renewal**
  Outdated technical narratives are not carried forward by default; courses are expected to track changes in real technical structure.

## Course-Design Style

- Judge first whether a course has already fallen behind.
- Reorganize it around the themes that actually matter, rather than stopping at small incremental fixes.
- Connect knowledge into paths through exercises and projects.
- Avoid letting toolchain detail overwhelm the concepts themselves.

The repository structures also reveal two stable features:

- a preference for week-by-week learning chains with clear dependencies instead of loose stacks of lecture notes
- a preference for keeping notes, code, assignments, and solutions together so that understanding, implementation, and evaluation stay connected

## Public Course Materials

- Topic pages and the mathematical-modeling tutorial page show that courses and series are often organized into public indexes and continue circulating beyond the classroom.
- Repositories such as `teaching`, `ss18-machine-learning-tutorial`, and `ws-18-19-deep-learning-tutorial` show that many course materials are maintained as reusable, updateable, and redistributable public assets.
- How those materials further enter communities, sites, and knowledge infrastructure is expanded in [Open Source, Talks, and Public Output](open-source-and-public-output.md).

## What Teaching Pushes Back On

- It forces existing knowledge to become systematic.
- It gradually turns breadth into depth that can actually be taught.
- It also enlarges value conflicts: what a teacher wants to give and what students are willing to carry are not always aligned.
